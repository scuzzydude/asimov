## A Digital Currency with Intrinsic Value

**or**

**a post-harden rockets cathartic**

There are only two true hedges against inflation. Recorded history and archaeology have proven they've both existed for as long as humanity.

Not real estate. That's a good one, as Mark Twain is to have said, "Buy land, they're not making it anymore." Elon Musk is trying to change that maxim, and I'm sure if Mr. Clemens were alive today, he'd have already bought his tickets to Mars. It's not true even on Earth. Tax Foreclosure, Floods, Texas Ice Storms, Hurricanes, Earthquakes, Mud Slides and Rising Sea Levels must be considered, plus don't forget War and Revolution can put bustles in your hedgerow.

It's not gold. I mean, come on, we tried that. Think gold is your savior as the world collapses? Good luck. Try throwing a gold bar at a zombie, see how that works out.

In the old days, if you had gold or land, it was most likely because you were born to it, or you took it. Wealth was bestowed onto you by your creator, either by giving you wealthy parents or by blessing you with the skills and fortune to win at battle.

In modern times, we feel that wealth is a result of humanity\'s creations. Certainly, through both the Industrial and Information Revolutions, it has been man's ability to innovate that has led directly to wealth and prosperity.

It follows that humanity should use its own creation as the fundamental measure of value. Floating currencies like the dollar and cryptocurrencies like bitcoin meet that criterion. It's ironic to me, that coders often rail against floating fiat currencies in comparison to the gold standard, as floating a currency is like the ultimate hack -- your algorithm doesn't change at all, you just comment out the line in the code that buys the gold. Bitcoin is a beautiful design, and pure in both intent and implementation. Neither dollar nor bitcoin represent any intrinsic value, and thus can't really hold value over generations and through calamity without intervention.[^1] Fiat and cryptocurrencies function as money -- a medium of exchange -- which is a technical function of a currency. Neither represent real value, which is the human concept of money. This disparity -- between the technical expression of value and the actual human appreciation of value leads to all kinds of crazy, like the price of bitcoin in the Spring of 2021.

The two true hedges against inflation are Genius and Vanity.

It is likely that within my lifetime, technology will have advanced to a stage where we can passively scan inanimate objects and digitize them down to the atomic level. 3-D printing will advance to a level where we can reproduce facsimiles of scanned items that and indistinguishable from the original to human senses.

It's very possible that in 20 years, you want a reproduction of the *Mona Lisa*, you pull up an app on your phone, pay the Louvre for a digital file, and 5 minutes later, your 3-D printer spits one out. It's likely that technology will allow you to print out a copy that matches what the painting looked like exactly when the paint first dried. Or in the year 1756, 1825 or even 1911, just before it was stolen. Every molecule will be accounted for, both the pigment and the pollution.

This might seem cool, but I'm an engineer, not an artsy type. I can already download a hi-resolution digital photo of old Mona and use it as a screensaver. Art lovers may tell you being in the presence of an original masterpiece is different. My cynical opinion, as an engineer, is unless you want to get all mystical about it, a molecule accurate facsimile should be able to give you that same feeling.

Facsimiles are not the true potential. The true potential of this technology is that one day, we may be able to reproduce the painting brushstroke by brushstroke -- it is possible we'll be able to deduce not only Da Vinci's strokes, but from that, his height, the size of his fingers, the strength of his forearms, the and the duration of the patience pauses between strokes down to the millisecond.

And then, just maybe, we might be able to understand Da Vinci's genius. Not everybody. Those who love his art, those who have the patience to watch a master paint for hours without checking the price of their dogecoin every five seconds. Perhaps Artificial Intelligence will be able to figure it out, and portraits will come back into fashion, a Leo robot for rent down at the Hobby Lobby.

Works of art don't just have historical value. They have potential value. And anything that is one of a kind has vanity value.

Genius and Vanity are intangible, but real. This is one problem people have understanding money. Most people can understand equities, a share of stock is partial ownership of company. Most people can understand a fixed currency - a gold-standard dollar is an actual gold coin. People used to understand that a paper dollar bill was backed by gold locked down under Fort Knox.[^2] But the floating dollar? That's backed by the "full faith of the United States Government" which I would argue is neither tangible nor real. I mean, the US Government consists of people we elected, I don't think I need to make a list of examples of all those faithless hypocrites. And bitcoin? Backed by mathematics? Mathematics is a form of genius humanity has created collectively through history, it's as much of an abstraction as faith in a government.

Genius and Vanity are timeless.

Genius is timeless. I don't mean to imply that if we put old Leo in a time machine and brought him to the year 2021, in a week he'd be rocking an iPhone while talking smack at crypto developers, puffin' on a vape pen, giving Banksy a run for a money spray-painting a masterpiece portrait of Queen Bey on the side of a railcar. No, he'd probably be spooked to death and think he was surrounded by sorcery. If I could talk to him, engineer to engineer, I think he would be able to understand Boolean logic, the basis of digital logic, and then quickly be able to fathom how simple but extraordinarily complex all these wondrous machines are. He'd understand it, not sure he'd believe it. His next stop would probably be either the confessional or the liquor store.

Vanity is timeless as well. We all know of people who like to name things after themselves, sometimes when they didn't have anything to do with creating it in the first place, sometimes when the creations weren't even that fabulous to begin with. I am talking to you, Mister Toyota Center, we all know that is the house *The Dream* built.

# The Foundation

In Isaac Asimov's *Foundation* series, a scientist named Hari Seldon uses advanced mathematics to predict the future and foresees the collapse of the Galactic Empire. His calculation shows him the collapse will last 30,000 years. He convinces the emperor that if he forms a Foundation to create the *Encyclopedia Galactica*, a complete collection of all human knowledge, and protects by hiding it away on a planet in a remote part of the galaxy, the collapse will only last 1,000 years.[^3]

Basically, Asimov was prophesying a planet where they have Wikipedia, the Internet, Amazon Prime Music, Video and Kindle when he wrote his stories in the 1940s, disguised as wildly speculative fiction set thousands of years in the future. His genius turned dreams into reality -- the Internet just taking off at the end of his life.

In *Foundation*, Asimov's reminding us that history always repeats itself and humans always forget history. Ask any crypto-crazy what he remembers about the years 2000 and 2008? If he says Lakers and Celtics, he gets a gold star but maybe he should think about cashing out. Unfortunately, worse things than NBA re-runs and financial corrections happen when humans forget history. Sometimes, it can be ugly and bloody.

What if we could turn history into money? Would we still forget?

**The Foundation** is a Non-Governmental Organization (NGO). It may be decentralized, but it's not autonomous. It's an ideal, not an algorithm.

Specifically, it is *The Foundation for the Appreciation of Human Genius*. The measure of that appreciation is in units of the digital currency **asimov**. This has a double meaning -- because for **asimov** to appreciate, humanity must appreciate the underlying works of genius. That means humanity -- especially our young -- needs exposure so they can appreciate the genius. The other sense is appreciation of **asimov's** market value relative to other currencies over time.

**Asimov** is a digital currency minted by **The Foundation** and initially circulated in exchange for **scoot**. **Scoot** are a class of digital token representing **proportional responsibility** for artwork or a legacy of genius.

If you're proposing a new currency that has the power to spread freedom and peace and promote equality, it should have a purpose. It should not be an algorithm. Ultimately, that is the reason I think many cryptocurrencies will fail over the long-term. They are trying to solve all the right problems, but what does the currency represent? Currency represents value. What we -- collectively and individually -- value. That is something that we need to be able to write down if we are going to have peaceful economic prosperity between peoples. It worked for many years when God spoke to a king who spoke to his people and said what we value is gold. Now we have different cultures, and we have different gods and different kings and different values. There is one thing that all cultures value, and that is genius. We almost universally value genius, even in genius in other cultures. One expression of genius is the physical expression of art -- artwork. I'll use artwork generically to define physical expressions of creativity.

The concept applies to all expressions of genius such writing, music, film, inventions and any kind intellectual property (IP). Those involve rights to expression of genius, and for the most part, fall into the public domain sometime after the creator dies. What consists of ownership of IP differs throughout the world and are much harder to quantify. Genius expressed in physical form is almost universally considered property.

Physical property rights are simple to understand. If you own something physical, you have the right to sell it. And if you own a proportional share of it, you get the proportional proceeds. Same goes for income earned for rental or production from that property. Once you own it, you can do with the property what you want if you obey the laws, including taking it somewhere else. Genius can be expressed physically. Property can be used as a basis for value.

I believe that one thing we value universally is genius. Across cultures. There is cultural bias of course, and cultural norms that keep us from admitting it. I love Dennis Rodman, and Kim Jong Ill loves Dennis Rodman, and Dennis Rodman loves Dennis Rodman. All three of us love Dennis Rodman, even though I want neither man around my family, it is because we all value a rebounding genius.

Not all art is genius. I'll venture to say, most art is not genius. Hey, I'll even say, most art is garbage. Here's the thing -- valuation of art is always individually subjective. That's why a market-based valuation is the only way to go. It is why art is sold at auctions.

The reason I use "expressions of genius" is I am aware that I individually and I as a member of 21^st^ century humanity, I may not be able to recognize genius. What I will recognize is that artists are attempting to express genius and my decedents may be able to recognize it, so it is my responsibility to preserve it for them to judge for themselves.

**The Foundation** is governed by a charter. This charter spells out the responsibilities of **The Foundation** and the principles which guide it. It should be a living charter, amendable and fluid, but guided by foundational principles. It should be written and voiced by young and idealistic people committed to a better world, not by a jaded cynical engineer who could not pick a Rodin out of a line-up of lawn gnomes.

The charter should begin with a simple principle. Genius belongs to Humanity.

I think this is a principle that will gain universal acceptance. There is already precedence for this. For Intellectual Property - immaterial expressions of genius covered by copyright -- we have the public domain. Some period of time after the creator of work dies, the rights fall to the public. This allows income from this IP to revert to heirs but does not perpetuate beyond the expiration of copyright or patent. This seems fair, as the creator and his family benefit, but the long-term appreciation of the value of his genius benefits humanity.

Think of physical art. A painter sells a picture for a large sum, maybe \$50,000 and it becomes property of some billionaire[^4] collector. Appreciation in value due to the painter's genius is conferred to the billionaire, not the painter or his family, and not to the greater benefit of all of humanity. However, the appreciation in value is due to humanity's appreciation of the genius, not the billionaire's vanity.

Creators of IP -- writers, musicians, filmmakers, digital designers and even coders, generally feel they get the short end of the stick compared to the creators of physical goods, because their work is difficult to protect from copying -- the classic double spend problem. In terms of extracting value from vanity this easily duplication allows them to have the ability to completely fulfill any level of short-term demand. Fashionable IP artists -- genius, the highly talented, the mediocre and the awful - can extract large amounts of value in the present, as well as the appreciation value of their potential genius for a couple of generations, depending on the medium.[^5]

The painter? He hopes that his work becomes fashionable in his productive lifetime, and that his genius is recognized while his family still has paintings to sell.

Works of art are property -- this is already universally accepted across cultures. Personal property rights are well established by history and have a great deal of commonality across cultures and legal systems. This system leverages that commonality.

We are establishing who should own art. Not legally, but voluntarily, because sharing the responsibility for genius collectively has a higher value for humanity than owning it individually. Over time, I think we can get to the point where universally we agree that human genius is the collective property of all of humanity. Every human who came before us, every human alive today, and every human that will be born in the future as a collectively "owns" human genius. Humanity's genius is our birthright collectively.

The Foundation will issue genesis **asimov** coins in exchange for **scoot**. **Scoot** are similar to coins or tokens. Unlike a coin or a token, scoot is a commitment of responsibility, not an asset. Scoot use the same protocol for exchange as **asimov**. Scoot are differentiated by an identifier, for instance, **Scoot.1**. In the protocol, scoot.0 represents **asimov**, where is **scoot.X** where, X is a positive integer larger than zero, and X is the identifier for a particular work of art or collection registered with **The Foundation**.

**Scoot** are minted and issued by a **scoot** **trustee (scootee)**. The **scootee** is a person for responsible for minting **scoot** and the legal disposition of a work of art or legacy. The **scootee** is an individual, usually the majority **scoot** holder elected for a term by the **scootage** (holders of **scoot**).

The **scoot trustee** may initially be a **creator**, **patron**, or a **crusade**. The **creator** is the individual or group who created a work of art. A **patron** either commissioned the original work from a **creator**, or has an agreement with the creator to protect the legacy of artwork. A crusade would be an individual or group that has a specific target, be it a work of art or to create a collection for a particular artist, subject or period. Each scoot has a **scoot charter (scooter)**, laying out the governance and may limit the rules of possession.

The scooter shall designate whether the scoot are **dilutable** or **non-dilutable**. The scoot has no means of distributing income to the **scootage**, however, the scoot may need to generate income from the scootage. An example would be a valuable painting held by a small local museum which has seen a dramatic drop in visitors. To properly secure and insure the painting, the scooter will dilute the scootage by minting more scoot in exchange for asimov, which can then be used directly or converted to fiat to pay for security and insurance.

**Scoot trustees** and the **scootage** do not own the artwork. This is in adherence to the philosophy that Genius belongs to Humanity, therefore artwork of genius should not be owned by an individual or an organization. However, **scootees** should hold legal title or lease of possession. The paradox of being the holder of the legal title to artwork but not ownership is one for the lawyers to work out, but it is key. Tactically, if the **scootee** holds the title, no one else can. A formal legal declaration should be drawn releasing title to humanity at large as part of the **scooter**, while keeping right of possession.

At **scoot** minting, the trustee assigns a curator for a term[^6]. The curator handles the possession, while both the **scootee** and **The Foundation** audit the curator to make sure proper care is taken of the artwork.

Holders of **scoot** bear a proportional responsibility for the preservation of artwork so present and future humanity can appreciate it. Alternately, since not every work of art is genius, **scoot** holders are responsible for preserving the fashion of today for the historical record of tomorrow. In either case, we can't say for sure which it is. An extremely high market value of **scoot** could reflect genius or reflect a present-day bubble of vanity. As a metric, the market value of **asimov** represents the cumulative historical value of genius and the present-day value of vanity.

This proportional responsibility for the work of art is the only "right" that the **scootage** are entitled to. They elect **scootees** and curators. They may vote on specific measure of handling or location of the artwork. From a legal perspective, I believe this could be important -- **scoot** are not securities because the underlying asset is not owned by the **scootage.** Scoot is the exact opposite of limited liability -- it is full responsibility. Responsibility does have value. In societal terms, proportional responsibility must have more value than limited liability. A system to measure that value has got to scare politicians everywhere. Good, it should.

Artwork that generates income will have to follow very strict rules. The **scooter** should spell out how much reserve a trustee should keep on account for insurance, security, maintenance and administration. Salaries may be paid to **scootees** but must be approved by the **scootage** and should be reasonable to the effort required. Since the primary responsibility of both **The Foundation** and the **scootee** is the appreciation of works of genius, excess income should be to fund programs that further the appreciation of the genius. Alternately income can be used to buy scoot on the open market, which decrease supply and increase unit value. Another alternative is to use excess income to create a collection of work. There is no means for excess income to be distributed direct to **scootage --** holding **scoot** is a commitment, not an investment. You can sell your responsibility, but you can only profit if you did your job, meaning the appreciation of the artwork grew. This means you were responsible. If your **scoot** fails, the valuation falls to zero, there is no way to get the artwork back.[^7] It belongs to humanity. At that point, possession would fall to the **The Foundation**, which would maintain the artwork and **scoot-chain** in **statis**.[^8]

**Scoot** can be openly traded for other **scoot** and for **asimov**, but not for fiat or crypto. This virtual separation of **scoot** trade is called the **responsibility domain**, where **asimov** trading for goods or other currencies occur on the **value domain**. Holders of **scoot** wishing to convert to fiat or bitcoin must trade their **scoot** for **asimov**, which can then be traded for other currencies. The scoot domain is a pure market of responsibility for genius and vanity -- not property -- we want to keep that separate from commerce.

One difference in the domains is that traders in the **responsibility domain** must be identified and validated by **The Foundation**. Trades in the **responsibility domain** incur no transaction fees. Traders in **asimov** on the **value domain** can trade pseudonymously like bitcoin or other cryptocurrencies, though this may require fees. I will describe a system will the overhead is low for non-anonymous traders. Registered traders in the **responsibility domain** are called **pledges**, because by holding scoot, they pledge responsibility for the appreciation of the associated genius. Only **pledges**, other scoot, or **The Foundation** may hold or trade scoot. There is no facility for corporations, government or any other kind of organization to hold **scoot** directly, though scoot may be formed as a collection of other scoot.

Since the process of minting **scoot** releases property rights to humanity at large, the underlying artwork ceases to be "private property." Essentially, trading **scoot** on the **responsibility domain** is a trade in appreciation of human creativity -- an exchange of opinions and not property. Monetarily, this appreciation can only be realized by trading **scoot** for **asimov**. An argument can be made those trades confined to the **responsibility domain** are not capital gains, though this is not the purpose of the arrangement.

The two domains have separate purposes. The **responsibility domain** is a market for the appreciation of human genius. **Scoot traders** are speculating in vanity and investing in genius. The difference in opinion is the arbitrage opportunity. The **value domain** establishes a metric, how much the sum total of human genius is worth to humanity in terms of commerce.

At establishment, the Foundation will mint one trillion **asimov**. The Foundation's purpose is the appreciation of human genius -- it does this by purchasing **scoot** in the **responsibility domain** and holding them. A division of the Foundation called the Encyclopedists[^9] are responsible for the appreciation -- in both senses. They act as researchers, providing **scootees** with advice and tools for establishing a digital data store, the scoot-chain, for each work of art, and interfacing with academia, and spreading public awareness of art. They are also buyers of genius and sellers of vanity.

**The Foundation** maintains the open-source software stack and applications used to trade **scoot** and **asimov**. Historical records of both trades in responsibility as well as research and in the case of IP, the actual works of creativity, are maintained on the **scoot-chain**. The Foundation will facilitate maintenance of at least one copy of each scoot-chain, but the actual responsibility for decentralized scoot-chain will be **staking agreements** between **scootees**.

The end goal of the Encyclopedist is that the Foundation hold all **scoot** of genius and none of vanity. This is an impossible task. Genius of today may be vanity of the future. However, in macro-economic terms, it gives us an equivalency. When **The Foundation** is "caught up" all the asimov will have been circulated, and the Foundation should hold in reserve 100% of the **scoot** of "genius."

**Scootees** are not obligated to sell scoot to The Foundation. The Foundation will establish barriers to entry to prevent a flood of novelty scooting. There will be at least two schedules of fees and the less expensive schedule will be if the scootee agrees sell scoot to The Foundation at initial minting. There will be a system of **sponsorship staking**, so that artistic attempts at genius have a path to participation without investment of significant capital.

At a hypothetical point in the future, when the Foundation has reached its end goal, this means that 1T asimov = the sum value of human genius. Some art will remain **un-scooted** and some of **scootage** will never sell all their scoot to the Foundation. Some forms of art will be difficult to scoot. It's an impossible task, but a worthy goal. The Foundation's **scoot** reserve holdings will always be public knowledge.[^10] In the early days, the price of asimov should be like an index fund of its holdings of **scoot**. Over time, as more asimov are issued for art, the market will adjust to valuating the asimov closer to the end goal -- a trillionth of the total value of human genius. All the asimov has been minted, and the only purpose it will be used for is genius appreciation, so the market should rise to the level of the potential.

In the **responsibility domain**, we have a non-commercial marketplace in genius and vanity. Art professionals can stay out of the Wall Street gutter, helping their clients build portfolios of genius, not collections of investments. It should amount to a gold rush in the Art World, without the need to sully their hands with the dirty money of the masses.

In the **value domain**, we have **asimov**, a metric of value that is common across cultures, geo-political divisions and the span of history. We will always value genius, even if we overcome vanity. Sure, some time in the future, we might be so far advanced, we may think Leonardo was a hack, but I'm pretty sure if that day ever comes, we won't need money.

### Reserve Stake

The purpose of the reserve stake is to provide a backing reserve in fiat and cryptocurrencies[^11] for **asimov** as a currency. The stake should not be touched, and conditions where the stake would be used or burned should be outlined in amendments to the foundational charter. There should be a long probationary period, and if **The Foundation** does NOT meet certain goals, the reserve stake should be returned to the stakers.

By staking the **asimov** early, we establish real world value for asimov issued for seeding and development. Development of this system will require time, work, and sacrifice from many idealistic people who desire a better world. There should be a large stake behind them.

1% of total **asimov** will be issued for staking.

#### Private staking **period**

The private staking period begins now and lasts until the stake reaches a threshold of \$100 million in USD equivalents. At that point the **distribution value** will be calculated. The average daily value of the stake currency in USD will be used between the time the stake is received until mint day. There will be a multiplier for each stake in the order it was received.[^12] Details of the private stake process are available here: <http://www.somethingfoundationrelated.org>

Staking will occur before the protocol is active, so initial asimov will be allocated and the splits will be communicated to the **staker** before the public staking period. Private stakes will remain anonymous to the public unless the **staker** makes it public and authorizes The Foundation to confirm their stake publicly.

The mint value of asimov will be established as the USD value of private state divided ½ of the total reserve stake (0.5% total asimov).

#### Public staking period

There will be a public staking period where the remaining 0.5% of asimov is sold in whole units at the **distribution value**. We want fair and geographically dispersed distribution, so asimov will only be sold in whole units, one at a time, one per individual. The Foundation shall determine a way to do this fairly, which will probably require some type of identification, perhaps this is opportunity for **pre-pledge registration**.

The purpose of the public staking is to prevent any one person from the private stake exerting control over of asimov and the development of The Foundation. The public stake is unconditional and shall not be returned, and may be used fund operational expenses. However, The Foundation will publish state of the public stake.

### National Stakes

10% of asimov will be reserved for national stakes. Once **asimov** is established as a stable metric, some asimov may be issued to national governments in exchange for holding stakes in their fiat currency or bonds. There may be legal concessions the national government must make before a stake agreement is made. For instance, they may be required to accept payment for taxes in asimov,

The Foundation's board shall decide when **national stakes** are both appropriate and feasible.

## Seeding

The Foundation will issue **asimov** to founders in exchange for seed money or work performed. People involved in the seeding of **The Foundation** should be signatories to The Foundation Charter. They may also **scoot** their artwork in exchange for pre-circulation **asimov**.

The Foundation is an extralegal non-national non-governmental organization (NGO). How you form one of those, I have no idea, but I'm sure it requires a lot of lawyers. Hopefully, the attorneys will take asimov in payment for their fees. Call me cynical, I sort of doubt it. I imagine the seeding will take substantial amount of fiat cash for this reason alone.

Since seeding will occur before asimov is available publicly, it is expected that all seed investors will provide a written and publicly recorded divestment pledge. Perhaps they pledge to sell their asimov on open market 10% a year for up to ten years.

The goal is appreciation of human genius, not capital. We're minting geniuses, not more billionaires.

Less than 1% of total asimov will be issued for seed capital.

## Development

Like most cryptocurrencies, asimov will be open source, and once launched, will be a community project. To get things off the ground we'll pay a small team in **asimov**. 1% of asimov will be reserved for development.

We're not trying to out-engineer the other thousands of cryptocurrencies. We'll take something that has been proven to work in terms of security and fit for our needs, branch, and run with it as close to the original codebase as possible.

Asimov will be a value currency. I'm sure there are legitimate uses cases for all the functionality and features incorporated into each and every new cryptocurrency. A lot of it is the same old Wall Street crap under a veil of decentralization. I can't see the point of much of it. Simple stuff like buy and sell options may be appropriate, but it must not be complicated. When it becomes too complicated to explain to a smart kindergartener, it is no longer a value currency.

For this reason, I think that the goal should be asimov as a base currency -- for both other cypto-currencies and for fiat currencies, and the protocol should have only the features needed to support that goal.

## Outreach Faucet 

The goal is to spread both interest and the idealism and seed the **scoot domain** with **asimov** capital. 1% of total **asimov** will be reserved for the outreach faucet.

### Art Professionals

The Foundation will exchange **asimov** for registration and training as **scootees**. Participants in the **responsibility domain** need a human entry point, and the existing network of Art professionals is the fastest way. Art professionals will be trained in the procedures of creating scoot. The foundation will stay out of the revenue side of it, but the expectation is the Initial Scoot Offerings (ISOs) will pay art professionals either a fee in fiat, or a portion of the ISO. Art Professionals trading their commission of **scoot** will create a liquidity pool of **scoot**, even when the trustee and initial holders have no wish to sell their interests.

It seems like this would also be a great way for galleries and dealers to reach out to their existing network of clients and artists and use momentum of the crypto craze to drum up business.

The Foundation should also fund and sponsor scoot exchanges primarily owned by art professionals. Since it's an open (but non-anonymous) network, the **responsibility domain** will likely draw interest from existing crypto-exchanges and spawn additional exchanges based on the currency speculation aspect. We want there to be exchange based on the value of genius, and need people who know their art to do it.

### Art Teachers

Remember that part about explaining how money works to a kindergartener? Well, we can either get a math teacher to explain the art part, or we can let the art teacher explain how a system a shared valuation works. My guess is that it's going to be a lot more fun with finger paint.

### Prizes

Another way to kindle interest in scoot is to award prizes for creative expression across all fields. Initially, The Foundation can award **asimov** for winners of a given prize. However, the contest itself will be the motivator for the associative genius, so I think we want to get to a point where the prizes are scoot. Prize winners are issued newly minted scoot, causing a dilution of the awards prior winners had been awarded. This forces a yearly raising of the bar and maybe the scootee does not award prizes in years when none of the competitors live up to the expectations of the prize.

## Museums

Perhaps we shall call this effort, *Bonfire of the Vanities y2.021k*. Before we open this section, it's worth a moment to reflect on the causes of revolution that occurred just over a hundred years ago, also in conjunction with a worldwide pandemic. It was a genesis revolution, leading to series of conflicts and societal shifts worldwide, and while these changes brought hundreds of millions of souls into the modern age, it also led to deaths of hundreds of millions, directly and indirectly. History is cyclical, but I'd prefer to think of recursively cyclical, getting closer to a viable solution with each attempt. This time, we have computers and the internet to run our revolution. If we can't make civilization better in terms of efficiency, at least there will be less paperwork.

I don't mean to pick on museums. This diatribe could just as easily be in the billionaire's section. ## Scoot of Agency

A **scoot of agency** (**SOA**) would work like this. A scoot is minted for a creative artist (with their participation) and the **scootee** hires an agent.[^22] This could be at the sole discretion of the scootee or an elective process by the scootage. The standard might be that the SOA receives twice the standard agent's percentage of an artist's work -- so for the example, 20%. This amount is highly variable, it just needs to be a greater percentage than what agents in that particular field of talent typically charge.

Income to the SOA is paid out to an agent at his standard rate, for an example 10%. This leaves 10% of the income held by the scoot. With that 10% the scootee may buy the scoot on open market, decreasing dilution which should increase the value of the scoot in terms of asimov. This is a function of income in the present. Or, based on the elective direction of the scootage (which in many cases will be in the majority control of the artist) it may keep some of the 10% as a reserve. An actor may use the reserve to finance his own film projects, or a musician may use it to free himself from dependence on a record label, and I guess artists buy paint and canvas.

We tie an agent's performance to legacy of genius when they are hired by awarding options to buy some amount of scoot at the price when they are hired, and right to sell those options back to the scoot at market price either at the end of a term, or when the relationship is terminated.

There will be two types of players in the trade of SOA. Speculators in vanity -- who feel the artist may not have long term genius but will have a high income for some period during their lifetimes. And investors in genius -- people who feel the artist will leave a mark on a culture beyond their lifetimes.

An artist with a liquid pool of scoot should be able to gauge what the public sentiment is from polling or formal elections by the scootage. Direction to decrease dilution would be an indicator that the world thinks his talent is transient. Direction to invest income in future projects would be a good indicator that the world thinks he might have some lasting genius.

Another consideration is who is a member of the scootage. The scootage are never anonymous to the scootee and The Foundation. If it's just fans or speculators, it's a pretty good indicator that an artist should focus on the present. If the **scootage** consists of other artists -- especially people the artist admires and respects, that's a good indicator that the artist should make careful career moves and focus on quality over income. If the **scootage** is held by peers it might mean there are collaborative opportunities. Let's use another hypothetical - If James Harden had a SOA, and the valuation is at an all-time high, he looks at the **scootage** and he sees that Keven Durant and Kyrie Irving have driven up his value by buying up the majority of liquid scoot, he should force his way out of town. *Adios*, dribble, dribble, dribble.

SOA give the artists tools to better judge his agent's performance in terms of income, career potential and legacy. Ultimately, an artist is independent of the SOA. Even if the artist loses majority control of the scootage, he or she can veto the choice of agents and always refuse work sourced from that agent. The scootee, who controls transactions of the SOA, can refuse to trades to pledges it thinks are counter to the best interests of the artists. It should be written into the scooter that the trustee can never refuse to trades back to the artist. If an artist loses control of the SOA, there is always a path to gain it back. If he or she has true genius potential, there will likely be a pool of resources that could help in that process. Even it's only vanity potential, an artist can crowdsource their way back to control through his or her fanbase.

Some may find it incongruent to add Agency to scoot and still call the responsibility domain non-commercial. I admit, it does seem that way. However, Agency is a balance of genius against the commercial side of creative talent. Every actor ever interviewed during a film promotion says he choose the role for the material, and never the money. Scoot of Agency will measure the accuracy of those kinds of statements.

### Pre-agency

First of all, optionality should be prime. Young people make mistakes. A young person's motivations and goals can change daily. A young persons should always have choices.

A pre-agency scoot (PAS) is a scoot formed for a minor by his parents or guardians, usually with a parent as scootee. PAS scoot are not tradable, and therefore have no value in asimov. The Foundation may take an interest in the PAS at no-par value. As a child develops his talent, the child and the scootee may award scoot to coaches, trainers, teachers, family members. Scoot awarded in this manner are a responsibility to look out for the best interest of the minor, so they need to be accepted by awardee formally through a confirmation transaction on the scoot-chain. While the scoot has no value, a **responsibility tree** is generated, so some evaluation of talent can be extrapolated from who holds scoot in a PAS. The PAS scootage can be consulted formally through elective process on the scoot-chain as to who should be hired as agents and professionals. The PAS itself does the hiring but it's a cash process, with either the parents of the child's income providing the payment. Agents may be hired on commission as in SOA, but hired professionals -- coaches, teachers, trainers - paid by the PAS should be forbidden from holding SOA scoot.

After reaching majority, a young person (artist) has the option of converting their PAS into a SOA. At that point, the artist can choose the conversion and dilution algorithm. He or she might choose a proportional conversion and dilution of PAS-scoot to SOA-scoot. Or they might issue token amounts a scoot in arbitrary manner. Or they may decide, all of those adults were in it for their own interest, and lock them all out. After all, a mentor they thought was scoot worthy at seven years old might turn out to be a real creep. The **responsibility tree** is discernable from the scoot-chain, may be hidden but should be shareable.[^23] How an artist treats those who helped them without any financial incentive to do so is a reflection on their character and their potential genius.

When The Foundation is a holder of PAS, it gives the option to the PAS-scootage to request a formal opinion. Say a young actor has a PAS and his parent scootee has chosen to hire an agent that has an unscrupulous reputation of putting children in exploitive roles. Perhaps the scootage believes there maybe kickbacks to the parents directly, or some other kind of shady business. The Scootage can create a petition for review, and The Foundation, as a holder of the PAS-scoot must form an opinion, which would require a formal investigation. The results of that investigation -- the unbiased findings of facts, would be published on the scoot-chain. This puts the scootee (likely parents) in a position to strongly reconsider, or at least publish their own due-diligence supporting their decision on the scoot-chain. In the USA, there would be nothing legally binding about the results of the petition, but it does create an immutable evidence trail for the minor which they can use once he or she reaches majority. Formalization of the decision-making process with the immutable records should add a level of scrutiny to the development of young talent that should reduce exploitation.

I know that this will be contentious area and one that will require a lot of thought and consideration. My purpose is just to spawn the development of these ideas and get lawyers thinking about how to solve the problems of protecting young talent proactively.

### Post-Agency

Where a Post-Agency Scoot (PAG) begins is the first question. Certainly, any scoot created for a writer whose rights have expired, would begin when a scoot is created. Digital recording is what it is. Yes, the studio masters have a higher fidelity than what is commercial released, but the master digital fidelity is the limit -- eternally. Technology for Analog to Digital (ADC) conversion continue to improve, year after year. Recorded music is not what drives this technology anymore -- the ADC we've had for years can pretty much cover anything the human ear can hear. However, there are all kinds of sensors that use ADC with much higher fidelity requirements and this technology will continue to improve, and that technology will naturally make its way into audio recordings. # Governance

The Foundations' method of governance shall be described and agreed to in the charter. The scope of The Foundation should be very limited.

The Foundation's scope is limited to the appreciation of Human Genius. The Foundation is charged with implementing a metric protocol so that it can measure appreciation, I've described an implementation of that in asimov and scoot. ## Electorates

A method of elections shall be built into the metric protocol.

### Responsibility Domain

#### Scootoro 

The **scootoro** is the sum total of all **scootees.** Each **scooter** shall define the method of voting by its scootage in regards to the scootoro. Representative voting means the elected scooter votes his own will. Common voting meaning each member of the scootage has one vote, regardless of their proportional holdings of scoot. Proportional voting means one vote per scoot held, and fractional votes for holders of fractional scoot.

#### Scootara

Every participant in the **responsibility domain** is a **pledge**. Pledges have registered with The Foundation and have credentials to trade scoot. Pledges who hold any number of scoot are members of the scootara, but they have but one vote, regardless of instances and number of scoot they actually hold.

### Value Domain

#### Asimass

The **asimass** are holders of asimov. Since asimov is an open digital currency, where holders may be anonymous and therefore may be individuals, corporations or governments, there is no concept of voting by individuals in the value domain. Large holders of asimov will dominate the asimass. Votes are proportional to asimov held.

## The Board of Trustees

The Foundation shall be governed by the Board of Trustees[^32]. I suggest that the total number of trustees be eleven, as follows.

### Engineer (1)

Since the metric of genius is an engineered system, we need an engineer to run it. The trustee engineer is responsible for asimov disbursement, as laid out in the disbursement plan, as well as stabilizing the metric. The Engineer also maintains the engineer's reserve, which is asimov leftover from the disbursement schedule. The engineer's reserve may be used in cases of emergency or crisis, and is passed on to the next elected engineer.

The engineer is elected by the scootara for a term of ten years.[^33] An emergency election is held if the engineer cannot complete his term, for the remainder of the term (decade).

The engineer's only direct tool is compliance with the disburstement schedule, which shall be part of the admeentments to the charter specificying the metric system. The engineer cannot exceed the disbursement schedule, but can disburse lower amounts than the schedule calls for and maintain an **engineer's reserve**, which can be carried over to the next engineer's term. The engineer can also disburse in uneven amounts throughout the terms.

# Origins

Apologies to Italy on this one, but the credit for the Italian-American spark that kindled my imagination is USA, all the way.

From the beginning of the pandemic, I'd been developing an idea with a friend that was a replacement for banks and the financial industry for businesses. Fundamentally, it used dynamic scalability for economies of scale. My fundamental model was what Amazon did for IT with AWS. I had quite a bit of reading of economic history as research.

For several years, my son had been trying to get me to invest in crypto, and as a coder, I understood bitcoin but was fundamentally skeptical. I certainly liked the decentralization aspect; I love solutions to problems that don't involve government. One the other hand, I had issue with the arbitrary creation of perceived value. Fundamental to the concept of money is the exchange of value. Bitcoin, gold standard and fiat currencies all fail in this regard, and so while I valued the cleverness of the implementation, I didn't think it didn't think they addressed the most important problem. I am not an idealist when it comes to my own money -- I wished I had listened to my son years ago. I think bitcoin was around \$300 when he first implored me to invest.

Coincidently, I'd been having long discussions with my daughter about all the social and political craziness we had to deal with in 2020 with the elections, protests and the pandemic. I blamed social media for of the hyperbole and hysteria. I also realized my generation had invented the technology that enabled all this but had not addressed the ethical problems that technology created. One of the core problems that was behind everything was wealth inequality. My attitude about this can be best summed up by Pink Floyd, "Money, it's a crime, share it fairly, but don't take a slice of my pie." I'm all for voluntary collectivism, spread the wealth, but I don't trust the government to distribute it. Both sides of the political spectrum had ideas about how to solve this, "Trickle down" or "Tax the rich" and neither had ever come close to addressing the core problem, which we both agreed stemmed from generational wealth.

After the first draft of the business idea, my friend implored me to add a crypto play. I was hesitant, as I didn't want to debase the fundamental idea. There was one technical problem with my system, and as it turned out, a crypto play was an elegant solution. In early 2021 I'd been doing a lot of study of crypto from a technical perspective. While I was impressed with the technology, I became even more skeptical of its societal value. About this time, my wife joined my son in the crypto-craze, and proceeded to multiply her initial investment by a hundredfold on paper. I'd just turned fifty, so it was as if the universe was telling me, "Give up already, you're old."

In April on the nights when I was free from my Rockets penance, my wife and I would watch some Netflix. For a couple of nights, we watched *This is a Robbery: The World's Biggest Art Heist*. This documentary was about the robbery of the Gardner Museum in Boston in 1990. Thirteen pieces were stolen and never recovered, including a Rembrandt and a Vermeer.

One theory was that the paintings were being held by a mob-boss as a "get out of jail free" card if he was ever arrested. This struck me as brilliant. That's some tremendous intrinsic value, that there was the belief that you could buy your way out of a felony charge with a painting.

Where did that value stem from? It was the appreciation of genius that was valuable. If you could quantify that appreciation, it could be a measure of intrinsic value. Then I realized that if you appreciate something, you would likely be willing to be responsible for it. If it really meant a lot to you, you would be willing to be responsible and accountable for it. Accountability was key. Our financial system is built around limited liability. This directly leads to limited accountability in politics and social matters as well.

# Ethical Revolution

Isaac Asimov isn\'t the OG of science fiction because he wrote about starships, robots, aliens and all kind of future technology, even though he did and pioneered the field. It was his consideration of ethics, specifically how science and technology impact human society. He is famous for postulating the The Laws of Robotics long before we had anything close to an actual robot.

It's a constant problem throughout history, our technology advances beyond the ability of our ethics. The industrial age led directly to industrial warfare, which killed hundreds of millions. It wasn't ethics that saved us from annihilating each other, but the ethical hack of mutually assured nuclear destruction. This hack hasn't saved us from bloody regional slaughter, child soldiers and genocide, but hey, at least the species has survived, and the arms industry is healthy.

Take the media. In my childhood, the standard local newspaper was considered a reliable conveyer of information. The front pages gave you facts. If you wanted a condensed version of the facts read to you by a white man, you could tune into one of the three major networks for local or national news. I'm not going to pretend that there wasn't both overt and hidden bias in the reporting in those days. But both newspaper and TV were profit motivated broadcasts, meaning they needed to appeal to the broadest audience. Newspapers could only slant maybe 10% one way or the other without taking subscription hits. The ethical standard for journalism had stabilized and improved over the years. You certainly take a newspaper from 1980 and compare it to a paper from 1880 and see that the standards for reporting "facts" had evolved for the better.

Not today. I don't think we'd even begun to sort the ethical complexity having Broadcast Media competing with Paid Cable News before we were hit first with the internet, then microblogging, then social media. In today's world, if you have an opinion, no matter how idiotic, about some recent or historical event, it will take you less than a second to find a confirming opinion and "facts" to back it up. How the heck do you deal with that ethically, while still protecting my first amendment right to be a bigger idiot than you? No idea, and it's unrealistic to expect that the founding fathers did either. However, it's not really their responsibility. We -- generationally -- invented the technology, and profited from it, so it's our responsibility to deal with the ethics of it.

Satoshi Nakumoto's brilliant engineering of bitcoin opened a whole new world for us, and allowed us to free our dependence on government and banks. Governments never did deal with the ethical side of money. Money is exchange of value. Warfare, and ultimately, any violent confrontation, is a difference in values between people and cultures. It seems to me, before we take our money completely into our own hands, we ought to find a common value across all people. It is simple -- if what you value is gold, people are less valuable. If what you value is hash rate, then both people and the environment are less valuable.

I made a crack about the US Dollar not being back by anything but the full faith of the government, which wasn't any better than bitcoin being backed by mathematics. Well, that's not true at all. The US Government is me. It's me and my parents and my children and my neighbors, Americans by birth or by choice. It's backed by our common values as Americans. As the year 2020 clearly shown, we are a divided people, we have extremes of opinions on multiple axes, and there are few things we can all get on the same page about.

There is one common value that Americans share, and we willingly share this value each and every human being on this planet, no matter where you were born or what passport you carry. This common value is embraced voluntarily and with conviction by 99.9% of all Americans and 100% of all Texans.

It is this value that gives me moral righteous to state, Genius belongs to Humanity.

Let's say I'm at a dinner party with a bunch of fancy artsy people.[^34] And some rich dirtbag makes mention he's got a Picasso hidden away at his ski-chalet in Aspen, and only the few other rich dirtbags invited on his ski trip ever get to see it. My response would be "Should I bow or curtsey, your royal fucking highness?"

While I fully concede to the rich their wealth gives them access to private jets, yachts and adventure flights into shallow outer space, I won't concede that they have the right to keep works of genius from me or my children. That's too much like royalty.

Our common shared value is that all men are created equal. No man by right of birth is better than another. We bow to no man, only to our creator.

Through the lens of history, we see that basic principle was hypocritical in practice, a perfect ideal tarnished by slavery, the genocide of indigenous people, and the denial of fundamental rights to our own mothers and daughters. Why is that?

A failure of the American Revolution, and all the democratic revolutions that followed, was that we said to the king, "We don't need you," but "We still need your gold." And that fact is why we, as a nation, could rectify slavery and genocide, economic imperialism and decade after decade of bloody industrial savagery with the idealism of liberty and freedom. All men are created equal, but from the day he is born, we value a person's worth by the same king's gold. We might have hacked the actual gold out of the algorithm, but it is still the king's gold be it fiat or crypto.

Hindsight is not perfect vision. I am not trying to fault the founders. I can't fathom how my parents survived childhood in Texas without air-conditioning, much less what a bunch of farmers and merchants in colonial American had to deal with. I do feel an opportunity was missed that was right there before their eyes. The very first amendment to our Bill of Rights says what we value is the freedom of speech.[^35] This has been interpreted from the beginning as freedom of expression, which is certainly what genius and vanity is all about. We as a people, demand this of our government, meaning we value it, and King George did not. Yet the founders choose to continue to trade and collect taxes using the king's gold.

And almost diametrically opposed and as great a failure were the collective revolutions that followed. In those systems, the king's gold was no longer what was valuable. It was the collective good that was valuable. Individual expression was not valued -- and core to individualism are both genius and vanity.

I believe mankind can learn to survive without greed.

I do not believe mankind can survive without genius and vanity.

Genius and vanity are really the same thing. Vanity is failure to align an expression of genius with the consensus of the masses. That does not mean one man's vanity is not genius. Galileo was the worst sort of vain person -- a heretic -- in his lifetime. Einstein, had he been born only a few years prior, probably would have ended up in a lunatic asylum, but by luck or fate or a divine game of dice, his very name across all cultures is synonymous with genius. Da Vinci might have been a genius in his day, but he worked for the billionaires of his time, vanity personified and glorified.

It used to be, for a leader to have moral authority, he had to do something substantially beneficial for people to take him seriously. Dude come down from a mountain, got him a list of ten rules he wants you to follow, your wife's all like, "Why the hell should we listen to him." And you're like, "Remember, babe, last year we were starving, getting whipped all the time, having to haul those huge ass stones all over the place for that dude with the big head, wears makeup all the time. Remember that?" She's like, "Well, I like that Sabbath thing, I could use a day off."

Today our decisions about our politics, investments, social morality and our environment are directly influenced by the random broadcast thoughts of billionaires and celebrities. This is our reality? Oh, can you imagine if Nikola Tesla's contemporary and compatriot, Mark Twain, had this level of source material to work with? That level of biting satire could have taken us directly from robber barons to Lennon's *Imagine*-land, skipping all the industrial warfare and bloody revolutions, saving a billion lives in the process. Give it another hundred years, this ridiculousness will be the Shakespearian in scope, and in a thousand years, will put every Greek tragedy to shame. We should listen to rich people about what how we measure value? Seriously? Maybe we really are in the Matrix.

A system that values genius is fundamentally sound not only for our peaceful coexistence with each other, but our coexistence with nature. I believe that potential genius is distributed in a statistically random fashion, the potential is there in children born in any country under any circumstance. Cultures that foster the development of those children in a safe, secure, healthy and sustainable environments will prosper. Cultures that raise children only as labor and consumers will fail. Genius is a renewable resource, while consumption of natural resources is not. Once we align ourselves to valuing creativity over property, we will place a higher value on sustaining an environment where creativity can thrive.

Wait a minute? Who's going to grow rice, and mow lawns, and paint my nails, if all the little ones spend all their time trying to be little geniuses?

Super-efficient robots that are powered by green energy. Don't believe me? Ask Elon Musk. How else are we going to colonize Mars? Pretty stupid to have super-robots on Mars and not use them here. Mars is a dangerous place. So is the earth for people forced by circumstance to lives of hard physical work.

I think that's what all these crazy tweets are about. I know Mr. Musk reads science fiction, and surely he's read at least one story where the children of the rich manage to escape the dying earth and go to Mars, or a space-station, or another star system, while the masses just float around on the ocean, starving, hoping a good looking dumbass farmer on a jet-ski will find them and take them to dry land. You think you're going to launch those rich kids from Texas? I'm not actually a big sci-fi geek, I don't keep up with everything, but I am almost sure there is a genre of "Mars as the Old West" or "Mars as a land of roaming samurai." If not, I hereby exert exclusive rights to all work in this genre, royalty negotiable. Yeah, you think we won't have property wars on Mars because like, we're only going to send nice people to Mars? Hold on here, I'm pretty sure all the water is in the same place.

I don't know if those crazy tweets are a direct message, or perhaps the Second Foundation on Trantor has used mental science to take control of Elon Musk's brain and send out these bizarre message. Fully deciphered, I think the message would be something like this: If you take the value system that we currently use on Earth to space you will also take war to space.

This is my plan. Stake the Asimov with a large amount of existing currency. Scoot physical artwork first, because the legal ramifications of owning and being responsible for property are well understood. Get lawyers working on an accountable method or sustaining proportional responsibility for a legacy of creativity across generations. Make the world a better place by having currency backed by things we value universally.
