## Part I: Identifying the Editors

### EdA (Editor A): v0.02 through v1.71

**Identified through:**
1. Lowercasing of Brandon's emphatic capitalized common nouns (systematic but inconsistent)
2. Light smoothing of argument flow without changing structure
3. Leaves comma splices intact (does not enforce semicolon rule)
4. Minimal footer/metadata work
5. Active in early-to-mid eras (1–5); absent after v1.71

**Evidence:**

- v0.2 → v0.21: EdA adds new argument sections ("The goal of this project is to change the way we value wealth...") and smooths transitions. Grammar fixes: "Isacc" → "Isaac," "with a few centuries the First Foundation" → "within a few centuries the First Foundation."
- v0.21 → v0.58: EdA is present but light-handed. Sentence restructuring preserves voice: "A value system built on something that is rare yet potentially unlimited..." → "...should result in less conflict and a more secure and sustainable economy."
- v1.05 → v1.63: EdA's work becomes minimal. Some pronoun fixes ("I am talking to you" → "I'm talking to you") and repetition removal, but voice is left untouched.

**Signature patterns:**
- "artwork" preferred over "artifacts" (consistency choice)
- Common nouns lowercased selectively: "Property of Wealth" (Brandon) → "property of wealth" (EdA), but not consistently
- Comma splices preserved: "It's important that The Foundation be, it's an ideal" (allowed to stand)
- Footnote management: Consolidates where appropriate, does not aggressively renumber
- No forced formality: Retains conversational asides and engineering cynicism

**Absence timeline:** EdA is not visible after v1.71. The editorial effort disappears into the October 2022 – May 2023 gap (v1.71 → v2.00). By v2.06, a different editor is dominant.

## Part IV: Detailed Editorial Patterns

### EdA's Approach: Invisible Editing

**Philosophy:** Edit in service of the author's argument, not for formality.

**Typical moves:**
1. Consolidate redundant explanations
2. Standardize terminology where author is inconsistent
3. Reorganize footnotes to improve flow
4. Light grammar: Fix obvious typos, retain voice
5. Never enforce formal rules: Comma splices left alone, conversational tone preserved

**Examples:**
- v0.21: EdA adds argument scaffolding that feels like Brandon's own voice. Seamless integration.
- v1.05: EdA removes Frank's Bikes, but the deletion preserves the logical flow. No text is left dangling.
- v1.63: EdA's final work is nearly invisible—just pronoun contraction and repetition removal.

**Why EdA fades by v1.71:**
The document reaches a mature state. There's less to refine; the argument is locked. EdA's role was to support the book *becoming* itself, not to police its grammar.

---

### EdB's Approach: Formal Standardization with Voice Preservation

**Philosophy:** Make the manuscript publishable without erasing the author.

**Typical moves:**
1. Comma splices → semicolons or periods (systematic)
2. Oxford commas inserted throughout
3. Table/image formatting standardized
4. Metadata organized (footnotes, image refs, publication dates)
5. **Critical:** Preserve colloquialisms, conversational tone, and engineering voice

**Examples:**
- "is making the choice; I'm going to invest" → "is making a choice. I'm going to invest"
  - EdB breaks the splice but keeps the conversational flow
- "Good Texan needs to know is; Question" → "knows is: Question"
  - EdB fixes the connector, doesn't rewrite the sentence
- "I ain't letting them park my car" → unchanged
  - EdB preserves voice even when non-standard

**Why EdB's pass is so large in v2.06:**
This is the moment Brandon decided to formalize publication. The Scoot Primer is added (pedagogical innovation). EdB is tasked with industrial-scale cleanup: prepare the manuscript for print without killing its voice.

**Why EdB's work continues through v2.13:**
Each version (v2.07–v2.13) shows minor adjustments, suggesting EdB is verifying the manuscript remains formatted correctly as Brandon makes small revisions. This is maintenance editing, not creative editing.

## Conclusion: A Collaborative Evolution

The editorial record reveals **two distinct editorial philosophies**:

1. **EdA (v0.2–v1.71):** A development editor. Works invisibly, supports the author's vision, preserves voice, and refines structure. Exits when the manuscript is mature.

2. **EdB (v2.00–v2.13):** A production editor. Makes the manuscript formally publishable, enforces consistency, handles metadata, and preserves voice despite formalization. Stays through publication.

Both editors respected Brandon's voice and argument. EdA focused on helping the book become itself; EdB focused on helping it become a professional publication. The manuscript shows **no editorial conflict**—these are not competing visions but complementary phases of a single work's evolution.

The presence of two distinct editors also explains why v2.06 feels like a pivot: EdB introduces the Scoot Primer (pedagogical reorganization), which is a **structural suggestion** from a professional editor who recognizes that readers need a clearer onramp to scoot mechanics. This is not a change imposed against Brandon's will; it's a recognition that the book's argument (complete and correct) needed a clearer presentation for its intended audience.

By v2.13, the manuscript is:
- Structurally complete (EdA's legacy)
- Formally polished (EdB's legacy)
- Authentically voiced (both editors' respect for the author)
- Ready for the world
