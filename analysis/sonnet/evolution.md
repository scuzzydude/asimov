## Part One: The Pre-Numbered Era (v0.01–v0.193)

### What These Versions Tell Us

The pre-numbered versions (v0.01 through v0.193) are drafts that predate the formal versioned series. They show the book in its raw, working state — before Brandon established the version discipline that begins at v0.2.

**v0.01 (691 lines)** is where the whole edifice originates. The core thesis is already fully formed and recognizable: "The two true hedges against inflation are Genius and Vanity." The opening argument (ruling out real estate, then gold) is already in place in almost exactly its final form. The Toyota Center/"house *The Dream* built" line is there. Da Vinci's brushstrokes, the Hobby Lobby Leo-robot, the confessional-or-liquor-store line — all present in v0.01.

What is absent in v0.01 and present later: the formal "A Measure of Wealth" section as a standalone chapter, the Twain/Tesla worked example as a structured argument (v0.01 rushes it), and the "Awbrey Hack" framing. The $100M private staking scheme is present in v0.01 in explicit form — this will be removed by Era 7 after SEC/Ripple concerns.

Also absent in v0.01: the Dennis Rodman line. It appears in v0.05 — "I love Dennis Rodman, and Kim Jong Ill loves Dennis Rodman, and Dennis Rodman loves Dennis Rodman. All three of us love Dennis Rodman, even though I want neither man around my family, and it's because we all can recognize a rebounding genius." This is one of Brandon's earliest and most economical proofs-of-concept for universal genius appreciation.

**v0.193 (1,222 lines)** is nearly double v0.01 in length. The growth happened through iterative addition to the specification sections and the circulation/staking mechanics, but the voice is still rougher than v0.2. Typos persist ("ALWAS BE DELUATBLE" remains in the italic technical spec stub, which seems intentionally rough-draft). The coder's voice is explicit in v0.01 in a way it will be toned down by v0.2: "floating a currency is like the ultimate hack -- your algorithm doesn't change at all, you just comment out the line in the code that buys the gold." This line survives in v0.2 but is gone by the time of the later versioned series — Brandon decided that the explicit code metaphor worked better embedded than foregrounded.

**[CODE-PARALLEL]** The pre-numbered versions function exactly like the early commits in a codebase before anyone adopts a commit message discipline. The architecture is already correct; the documentation is rough; the unit tests (footnotes) are thin.

### The Document's Original Scope

In v0.01, the book is primarily about *physical art*. The word "artifact" recurs throughout the early spec as the unit of value — "artifacts of genius." The Scoot system was originally designed to govern the stewardship of physical art objects: paintings, sculptures, prototypes. The broader concept of "ideas" — encompassing music, writing, sports achievement, historical moments — is present but secondary.

By v0.2, "artifacts" has become "works of art" and then "expressions of genius." By Era 2, the system has expanded to govern anything that can be appreciated: songs, basketball legacies, environmental solutions, software. The physical-art frame has become a founding example rather than the entire scope. This conceptual expansion is the true story of Era 2.

## Part Two: Era 1 — Conceptual Rewriting (v0.2–v0.31)

### The Book's Argument in v0.2

The book in v0.2 argues: physical art is the most demonstrable form of stored genius; blockchains can make stewardship of art equitable; a currency backed by the responsibility for genius appreciation can replace property-backed currency. The Twain/Tesla worked example appears in v0.21 but is still rough — the punch line "we will value responsibility" is present but not yet the crisp declaration it becomes.

v0.21 is the most significant single step in Era 1. The "A Measure of Wealth" chapter gets its standalone argument for the first time: "The goal of this project is to change the way we value wealth." The Genius Economy frame — property-based wealth vs. genius-based wealth — crystallizes here as the book's organizing opposition.

Also in v0.21: the first appearance of "Responsibility is what is valuable" as a standalone sentence. This will become "Passion is all that is required" by v2.13 — a small refinement that makes the thesis more inclusive and emotionally accessible.

**[CODE-PARALLEL]** Era 1 is the refactoring phase: no new features, but the internal architecture is reorganized so that later additions have a clean place to plug in.

## Part Three: Era 2 — The Great Expansion (v0.31–v0.58)

### The Doubling

The document grows from ~2,400 lines to ~3,949 lines between v0.31 and v0.58 — a 65% increase. This is the book's most significant creative period.

What gets added in Era 2:
- The full Houston sports narrative (Hakeem Olajuwon as genius-vs-vanity proof of concept; the GOAT debate; the 1994 championship as mythology)
- The Drumland section (musicians and the genius economy)
- The Collecting Responsibility section (Scobee postcard, the signed basketball eureka)
- Substantial expansion of the Billionaires section
- The Texas History material (ancestors of Appendix E)
- The Digital Sainthood and Stasis chapters
- The Altuve/Asterisk material
- Multiple new footnote types including timestamp/karmic update notes

Era 2 is when the book discovers it is not just a whitepaper. The Houston basketball narrative, the Drumland story, the Scobee postcard sequence — none of these are economic specification. They are memoir and argument intertwined. The crypto advisor subplot emerges here. The Canada running-joke begins.

**[CODE-PARALLEL]** Era 2 mirrors the `#if 0` accumulation phase in the astros repo: the code base grows as new modules are added without removing old ones. The specification sections from v0.01 are still intact; the new human narrative material is added around them. Nothing is deleted, only accumulated.

### What the Expansion Proves

By v0.58, the book's implicit argument has shifted from "here is a crypto system" to "here is why a person from Houston in 2021-2022 understands value better than most economists." The Rockets narrative is not decoration — it is the primary proof of the thesis. If Hakeem Olajuwon (genius, equitable, refuses to sell his name for vanity) is more valuable to Houston than Michael Jordan (vanity, commercial, brand-first) would have been, then genius > vanity is provable.

## Part Four: Era 3 — Expansion and Argument Refinement (v0.58–v1.05)

### The ARC Appears

Somewhere in Era 3, the book acquires the "ADVANCED READING COPY" label (visible at v0.95). This is a significant marker: Brandon is now thinking of the book as a publishable object, not a specification draft. The ARC label signals that real copies are circulating to real readers.

Era 3 continues the expansion of Era 2 but the new material is more argumentatively integrated. The Genius Economy concept gets a fuller treatment. The Scoot Primer section that will arrive in v2.06 is anticipated here by the increasingly reader-oriented framing of the technical sections.

**The $100M Staking Scheme** survives Era 3 intact. It will be killed in Era 4 after the legal consultation with the crypto advisor (the SEC/Ripple concern). In Era 3, the scheme is the book's proposed launch mechanism: stake the Foundation with $100M in property, hold for 10 years, stakers can reclaim or keep asimov at the end.

## Part Five: Era 4 — Section Pruning and Steve Fairchild (v1.05–v1.63)

### The Frank's Bikes Excision

The most significant cut of Era 4 is the Frank's Bikes worked example, deleted between v1.05 and v1.63. Frank's Bikes was a multi-paragraph illustration of why "property of wealth" must stay out of the responsibility domain — a bicycle manufacturer's rubber supply becoming a speculative asset. The passage was legally and conceptually thorough but slowed the book's rhythm. By v1.63, a single sentence replaces it: "Ultimately, determining what is Property of Wealth and what is just property is the responsibility of the trustee and the scootage."

**[CODE-PARALLEL]** This is the vtable simplification: complex implementation hidden behind a clean interface. The caller (the reader) gets one clean sentence. The complexity is now the trustee's problem, not the reader's.

### The $100M Stake Becomes "First Foundation"

Also in this era, the $100M private stake proposal is reframed as "First Foundation" — a historical description of what the first edition proposed, and why Second Foundation supersedes it. The SEC/Ripple concern is cited explicitly. Brandon's response: "why poke a pig in the eye?" The system is redesigned to depend entirely on ideas (scoot) rather than property (fiat currency) as the staking vehicle.

This pivot is one of the most important conceptual moves in the book's history. The original v0.01 system required property to bootstrap — it was a system that still depended on the king's gold to get started. Second Foundation eliminates this dependency. The system is now recursively pure: ideas fund ideas.

**[CODE-PARALLEL]** This is recursive integration applied to the book itself. Brandon finds a hypocrisy in his own design (claiming to eliminate property while requiring it for launch) and recurses upon it.

### Steve Fairchild: The Late Addition That Changes Everything

The Steve Fairchild tribute and dedication is added entirely between v1.05 and v1.63. In the diff record, it appears as a block of fresh text — over 1,500 words — with no deletion counterpart. It was written as a standalone addition.

The passage describes Steve Fairchild, the engineer who most influenced Brandon's career at Compaq/HP during the development of SAS/SFF HDDs. Steve's lesson: "The methodology is the most important thing." Steve died in 2013 without Brandon having told him what he meant.

Why does this passage appear at Era 4, and not Era 1 or 2?

The answer is structural. By Era 4, the book has established its thesis, proven it through basketball analogy, worked it through the Houston narrative, and created a full governance specification. What the book still lacks is a direct statement of why *this particular engineer* was qualified to propose *this particular system*. Steve Fairchild is that statement.

Brandon's methodology argument — that measurement frameworks matter more than the numbers they produce — is the core of both his engineering career and his economic proposal. The Scoot system is not an investment vehicle or a speculative play; it is a measurement system. "The Foundation is a measurement system for intrinsic human value." That is Steve Fairchild's lesson applied to civilization.

The dedication ("This work is dedicated to Steve Fairchild") is the last line of the Fairchild section and the last substantive addition before the book enters its tightening phases. It functions as a structural anchor — everything before it was building toward this claim of authority.

## The Argument: What Changed from v0.01 to v2.13

**v0.01 argues:** Physical art objects represent trapped genius value; if we create a system to govern stewardship of art without property claims, we can create a currency backed by genius appreciation. The staking scheme requires $100M in fiat to launch.

**v2.13 argues:** All human genius — art, music, sports achievement, scientific discovery, entrepreneurship, environmental solutions — can be measured through a system of proportional responsibility. Scoot is the unit of that responsibility. Asimov is the metric of its aggregate value. The system requires no property to launch and is designed to be recursively pure of property claims from inception. The Awbrey Hack is to make money from the only thing that is truly valuable: humanity's imagination.

The thesis changed in one crucial way: the system went from *governing existing genius* (stewardship of art) to *creating new genius* (enabling dreamers who currently lack property to build genius wealth). This is the democratic pivot. In v0.01, the Scoot system benefits art collectors and the cultural establishment. In v2.13, it benefits anyone with a passion and anyone who appreciates someone else's passion.

## Code-Parallel Synthesis

The evolution of *Asimov* over 110 versions mirrors the 326-commit arc of the astros repo in four specific ways:

**1. The API never changes.** From v0.01 to v2.13, the thesis is identical: genius and vanity are the only true inflation hedges; build a system to measure them. Every implementation — staking scheme, governance structure, worked examples — is an impl that plugs into this unchanged interface.

**2. No code ever truly dies.** The Frank's Bikes passage was cut in Era 4, but its argument (keep property out of the responsibility domain) was not lost — it was compressed to a single sentence and the complexity moved to the trustee's charter. The $100M scheme was cut, but reappears as "First Foundation" — an `#if 0` block that is preserved as documentation. The Texas History appendix contains the Indian burial mound story stub (`...Grandpa indian burial...`) in rough outline that eventually becomes the fully written Big Mama / Little Mama narrative.

**3. Measurement methodology over implementation.** Steve Fairchild's lesson is Brandon's engineering credo and his book's conclusion. The methodology (equitable measurement of genius) is more important than the implementation (the specific protocol). This is why the spec sections move to appendices — the methodology is the book.

**4. The mortal engineer problem.** Steve Fairchild was the best engineer Brandon worked with and is dead. This is the crisis the book addresses obliquely: what happens when the engineers die and the methodology dies with them? Blockchain's immutability, the scootchain as digital stone, the Foundation as the Encyclopedia Galactica — all of these are Brandon's answer to the same problem that killed Asimov's Galactic Empire. Great engineers die; their methodology must outlive them.
